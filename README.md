# Hold the Isle

**Hold the Isle** is a scrolling, turn-based campaign in the manner of Chris Crawford’s *Eastern Front (1941)*. Two campaigns share the Channel map:

- **The Year of Three Kings- 1066.** You are William of Normandy. Harald Hardrada is already in Yorkshire. Harold Godwinson holds London.
- **Operation Sea Lion- 1940.** You are the German invasion command. Britain holds the island. The barges do not sail until the air is won, and the Royal Navy is waiting.

Repo: [github.com/rkosinski941/HoldTheIsle](https://github.com/rkosinski941/HoldTheIsle)

## Play

Serve the folder (modules will not load from `file://`):

```
python -m http.server 8080
```

Open `http://localhost:8080`. On the title screen, choose the campaign, then the difficulty.

- **1066** — Cross the Channel. One turn is four days, from 18 September to Christmas.
- **1940** — Launch Sea Lion. One turn is four days, from 15 September to 10 November.

## 1066

You command William of Normandy. Harald Hardrada is already in Yorkshire. Harold Godwinson holds London. The Channel weather will not wait, and neither will the English fyrd.

## How 1066 works

- One turn is four days, from 18 September to Christmas 1066.
- You plot up to eight cardinal orders per unit. Orders persist if unused.
- All three sides then resolve together across 32 ticks. Terrain, weather, and enemy zones of control delay or halt a march.
- Combat is a series of small blows, not one roll. Units break, then shatter.
- Fleets carry up to three land units. Gales close the Channel.
- Take London, and either Winchester or Harold’s death.

## 1940 — Operation Sea Lion

You command the invasion. Britain’s Home Forces, Home Guard, Fighter Command, and the Royal Navy are played for you.

- The armies muster on the French coast: 16th Army toward Dover, 9th Army toward Sussex, with the barge flotillas, a thin destroyer escort, JG 26, JG 2, KG 2, and Student’s 7th Flieger.
- Brooke, Montgomery, XII Corps, and the Home Guard hold the south. Park’s 11 Group starts with the air. Ramsay is at Dover.
- Each turn the surviving fighters and bombers set the next turn’s air situation. **RAF superiority** keeps the barges and the paratroops from crossing. **Contested skies** make the crossing slow. **Luftwaffe superiority** eases it and harasses British movement in the south. A gale closes the sea either way.
- March a land unit onto a flotilla, then onto an English beach or a south-coast port. The troops step ashore there. 7th Flieger can cross without ships. Clicking a beach from France plots that crossing.
- Bombers strike ships and troops. The Royal Navy will not enter a German port, but it will fight barges at sea.
- Take London at any time, or still hold two of Dover, Canterbury, Hastings, Pevensey, and Chichester on 10 November. If the invasion army is destroyed, or the date passes without London or a lodgement, Britain wins.

What is simplified: one counter is a corps or a group, not a division’s paperwork; there is no radar, no separate build-up of barges, and no second front. The season ends in November.

## Keys

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Arrows / WASD | Move cursor (plot orders if a unit is held) |
| Enter | Pick up / put down |
| Click | Select, then plot a path |
| Backspace | Cancel last order |
| Space | Clear orders (skip animation while resolving) |
| N | Next friendly unit |
| T | Execute the week (1066) or the four-day turn (1940) |
| ? | Help |

Map data is generated by `tools/gen_map.py` into `data/map.json`.

A headless check of both campaigns, including a scripted Sea Lion landing and the victory tests:

```
node tools/sim_campaign.mjs
```
