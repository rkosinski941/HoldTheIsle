Anno Domini MLXVI

Hold the Isle

The Year of Three Kings- 1066

A scrolling campaign in the manner of Chris Crawford’s Eastern Front

How to play

You are William, Duke of Normandy. Issue orders to your host, then execute the week. English and Norse orders resolve at the same time as yours.

  • Move the pink cursor with the arrow keys or WASD. The map scrolls at the edges of the screen.
  • Enter picks up a Norman unit. Further arrow keys plot up to eight marching orders. Enter puts it down.
  • Click a unit, then click a destination, to plot a path. Backspace undoes one order; Space clears them.
  • T (or Execute week) runs thirty-two ticks of movement and combat. Space skips the animation.
  • March a land unit onto a friendly fleet at the coast to embark. Order it onto an English beach to land.
  • Gales close the Channel. Harold will march north on Hardrada first. That is your opening.
  • Hold London, and Winchester or Harold’s death, before Christmas.

You command the German invasion, 15 September 1940. Each turn is four days. Britain moves when you do. The barges cannot sail while Fighter Command holds the air, and the Royal Navy hunts them once they leave port.

  • N picks up the next German unit. Enter puts that unit down and leaves its orders on the map. Arrows plot up to eight orders.
  • Click one of your units to hold it. Click a beach, a town, or open ground to plot the path. T executes the turn; press T again to skip the animation, then release it before the next turn. Space also skips.
  • The grey margin is out of play. Orders will not step onto it.
  • Troops step ashore when the barges reach a beach or a south-coast port. 7th Flieger can cross without ships.
  • Fighters contest the sky. The air situation for the next turn is the balance after this one. Bombers strike ships and troops.
  • Gales close the Channel. RAF superiority keeps German barges and paratroops from crossing. Contested skies make the crossing slow.
  • Ramsay’s destroyers will not enter a German port, but they will fight barges at sea and in the harbor mouth.
  • Take London, or still hold two of Dover, Canterbury, Hastings, Pevensey, and Chichester on 10 November. Otherwise Britain wins.

The campaign is ended
